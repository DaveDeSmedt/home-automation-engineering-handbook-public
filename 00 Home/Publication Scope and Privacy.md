# Publication Scope and Privacy

## Purpose

This public edition teaches methods. It must not become a map, operating manual or attack surface for a real home.

## Removed or generalized

- Names of people, household routines and identity-specific examples.
- Addresses, geographical context, floor plans, photographs and exact property layout.
- Network names, IP ranges, device identifiers, serial numbers, tokens, passwords and recovery codes.
- Alarm, camera, lock, access-control and emergency-response configuration.
- Exact equipment inventory, installation positions, purchase history, warranty material and vendor-account details.

## Editorial substitutions

The book uses generic terms such as **Study**, **Transition Corridor**, **lighting platform**, **battery system** and **multi-room audio platform**. These are teaching constructs. They do not imply that a particular product is installed, supported or suitable.

## What remains deliberately specific

automation controller is named because it is the book's orchestration context. References to external product categories or vendor documentation are contextual and not endorsements. Readers must check their own hardware, app versions, local laws and manufacturer instructions.

## Safety boundary

Automation may improve comfort and visibility, but it must not replace required electrical protection, fire detection, physical security, medical safeguards, battery management or professional installation practices.

## Review rule

Before a public release, run a text search for names, addresses, IP addresses, credentials, serial numbers, proprietary screenshots and supplier documents. Treat uncertainty as a reason to remove or generalize.
